# Independent application audit: a pure [[45,1,7]] code

Facts: The proposed code is correct. An independent exact syndrome enumeration below certifies purity and distance seven without assuming the separate graph-distance computation. The construction itself is standard CWS theory and, conditional on the graph's six-uniformity, is an immediate instance of Sudevan et al., Theorem 2. No code-parameter optimality, priority, fault-tolerance, or locality of arbitrary logical encoding is claimed.

## Definitions and direct proof

Let G=C3 square C3 square C5 be the simple Cartesian product graph, with vertices (a,b,c) in Z3 times Z3 times Z5 and edges joining vertices differing by plus or minus one in one coordinate. There are 45 vertices and 135 edges, and every vertex has six distinct neighbors. Let A be its symmetric binary adjacency matrix, with no diagonal. The graph-state generators are K_v=X_v product_{u in N(v)} Z_u, with positive signs. They are independent, commute, and stabilize the graph state |G>.

The two codewords are |0_L>=|G> and |1_L>=Z_all|G>, where Z_all is the product of Z on all 45 sites. They are orthogonal graph-basis states. The classical code C={0,1^45} is the binary [45,1,45] repetition code, so their span has dimension two and is an additive stabilizer code. Its stabilizer group is S_even={product_v K_v^{x_v}: sum_v x_v=0 mod2}. Positive phases are inherited from actual products of the commuting K_v, so minus the identity is absent.

The 44 operators K_0 K_v, v=1 through 44, form one independent generating set. A geometrically local alternative consists of K_u K_v for edges (u,v) of any spanning tree of G. The incidence vectors e_u+e_v of a connected spanning tree span exactly the even-weight binary subspace, so this alternative generates the identical rank-44 stabilizer. The application script verifies both ranks and equality of their spans independently.

For a Pauli E represented modulo overall phase by (x,z), with Hermitian convention E=i^{|x intersect z|}X^x Z^z, its graph-basis classical image is c=z+A x over GF(2). Its syndrome against K_0 K_v is c_0+c_v. Thus it commutes with all code checks iff c is either zero or all ones. This formula is directly verified against the symplectic product for every single-qubit X, Y and Z in the program.

If one assumes graph-state distance seven, the proposed short proof follows immediately. A nonidentity Pauli of weight at most six cannot have c=0, because it would then be a graph-state stabilizer up to phase of weight below seven. Each occupied qubit contributes at most seven sites to c: X contributes its six neighbors, Z contributes itself, and Y contributes the union of those sets. Consequently wt(c)<=7 wt(E)<=42, so c cannot equal 1^45. Every nonidentity Pauli of weight at most six is therefore detected with zero projection onto the code. This is purity, not merely an absence of off-diagonal logical transitions.

A distance-seven witness is K_v itself. It commutes with S_even and acts with eigenvalues +1 and -1 on |0_L> and |1_L>, respectively. Thus it is a nontrivial logical Z of weight seven. A logical X representative is Z_all: it exchanges the two codewords and anticommutes with K_v. This weight-45 representative is not asserted to be minimum weight in its logical coset. All K_v represent the same logical Z because K_u K_v belongs to S_even.

## Independent exact finite verification

The program verify_code.py reconstructs the lattice from its Cartesian coordinates using integer bit operations only. It obtains graph-generator rank 45, star-check rank 44, spanning-tree-check rank 44, and rank 44 for their combined row space. All relevant pairwise symplectic products vanish. Appending the proposed logical X and logical Z gives rank 46, equal to the full normalizer dimension 2n-(n-1), and the logical pair anticommutes.

Independently of the graph-state distance claim, the program enumerates every Pauli error of weight at most three modulo global phase and requires all syndromes to be different, including the identity. The exact counts by weight 0,1,2,3 are 1,135,8910,383130, totaling 392176. All 392176 syndromes are distinct; there are zero collisions.

This finite test proves the required distance lower bound, not merely a random-error check. Any nonidentity Pauli F of weight at most six can be split on disjoint supports into E_1 E_2, where both factors have weight at most three. If F commuted with every check, the two distinct factors would have the same syndrome, contradicting the exhaustive enumeration. Hence no nonidentity Pauli of weight at most six lies anywhere in the normalizer, including the stabilizer subgroup. The code is pure and has distance at least seven. The weight-seven logical K_v proves exact distance seven. This also independently certifies the graph-state minimum stabilizer weight seven, since every graph stabilizer lies in the code normalizer and canonical K_v has weight seven.

The canonical ordered error/syndrome stream has SHA256 d2bb10d3513ff7b0fc394a59155857a409b4fb20ecf4f433c55a9a5d99132dd4. The stream order and byte encoding are recorded in verification.json. Complete adjacency rows, all 44 star-check rows and the spanning-tree edges are retained there; hundreds of thousands of individual error strings need not be stored because their exhaustive generation is deterministic. Runtime on the local standard-library Python was about .71 seconds. This is exact binary arithmetic and does not rely on statevector simulation or floating tolerances.

## Geometric consequence and source overlap

Sudevan et al., Codeword Stabilized Codes from m-Uniform Graph States, arXiv:2405.06142v4, Proposition 3 states the additive CWS construction from a binary linear code; Proposition 4 identifies its stabilizer subgroup. Theorem 2 states that an m-uniform graph state on an m-regular graph combined with a classical [n,k,d] code satisfying d>m(m+1) gives a pure [[n,k,m+1]] code. Setting m=6 and using [45,1,45], with 45>42, is exactly the present conversion. Their Proposition 2 and Corollary 1 certify periodic rectangular lattices only when every side is at least eight. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2405.06142v4 .

Consequently the defensible resource statement is geometric: the explicitly verified 3-by-3-by-5 periodic lattice supplies the required six-uniform graph state on 45 sites, whereas the smallest three-dimensional rectangular lattice covered by that particular published side-at-least-eight sufficient guarantee has 512 sites. This is a comparison of the graph-state resource required by that theorem's stated sufficient conditions, not a comparison with all known [[n,1,7]] codes or all possible quantum-code constructions. The graph resource uses 135 nearest-neighbor edges on the periodic topology. Spanning-tree stabilizer checks have weight 10 or 12, with support contained in the union of the closed neighborhoods of two adjacent vertices. The specific verified tree has 40 weight-10 and 4 weight-12 checks. These facts do not establish an ancilla layout, fault-tolerant measurement schedule, or a local circuit that encodes an arbitrary logical input.

Cross, Smith, Smolin and Zeng, Codeword Stabilized Quantum Codes, arXiv:0708.1021, Eq. (10) gives the graph-induced classical error map; Theorem 3 gives the detection criterion including zero-image degeneracy. Section III.A already uses the two repetition word operators I and Z_all for cycle-graph codes. Thus the repetition-CWS idea is established. Source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/0708.1021 .

Kovalev, Dumer and Pryadko, Design of additive quantum codes via the code-word-stabilized framework, Phys. Rev. A 84, 062319 (2011), Examples 10 through 12 discuss repetition-based additive/cyclic CWS constructions and regular-lattice code structure. They provide further antecedents, not a checked identification of this particular 45-site Cartesian torus. Source: https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevA.84.062319 .

The 2019 JMM abstract Combinatorial Properties of Diagonal Distance explicitly announces a study of Cartesian graph products and diagonal distance. Its complete result remains unresolved in the parent review, so priority of the geometric graph-distance claim is not established. The official abstract collection contains that statement: https://jointmathematicsmeetings.org/meetings/national/jmm2019/JMM_2019_abstracts_journal.pdf . This application audit does not turn an unresolved priority question into a novelty claim.
